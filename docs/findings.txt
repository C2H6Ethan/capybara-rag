Initial corpus analysis revealed that 7 of 12 source articles had fewer than 1 capybara mention per 1000 words. Indexing these without filtering would mean the majority of chunks contained zero relevant information. I implemented paragraph-level filtering to retain only chunks with direct capybara relevance, which improved retrieval precision significantly."


After filtering non-capybara content, the Wikipedia-sourced corpus contained only ~4,755 words (~20-25 chunks). This is insufficient for meaningful retrieval evaluation. A RAG system with 20 chunks has no real search problem — you could fit everything in a single prompt. Target corpus size is 200-500 chunks, requiring dedicated capybara-focused sources rather than general Wikipedia articles about related topics.

Final filtered corpus: 14,313 words across 17 files. Raw corpus was 27,229 words — filtering removed ~47% of content as non-capybara noise. Largest contributors: a-z-animals (3,740), capybara_main (2,137), thesprucepets (1,830), animaldiversity (1,441). Corpus covers biology, habitat, diet, behavior, reproduction, predators, conservation status, and care/husbandry.
Retrieval quality assessment on 5 test queries: weight query performed excellently (distance 0.206, direct answer in top result). Diet query retrieved relevant but indirect chunks — top result described feeding behavior rather than specific foods. Care query (minimum group size) failed to retrieve specific guidance, suggesting care corpus lacks prescriptive detail. Predator query retrieved partially relevant results. Negative query (Antarctica) correctly retrieved geographic context enabling LLM to answer 'no'.
Observation: retrieval quality varies significantly by query type. Factual numeric queries (weight, size) perform best. Behavioral/care queries perform worst. This pattern likely reflects corpus composition — more descriptive natural history content than prescriptive care guidance.
